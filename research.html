What I'm chasing

I search for dark matter — the invisible substance that dominates the matter content of the Universe — with liquid-argon detectors deep under the Gran Sasso mountain.

Hunting dark matter with DarkSide-20k

I work on DarkSide-20k, a next-generation dual-phase liquid-argon time projection chamber searching for WIMP dark matter at the Laboratori Nazionali del Gran Sasso. A dark matter particle would reveal itself as a tiny flash of light and a handful of ionization electrons — and the whole experiment is built to catch it against an almost perfectly quiet background.

Reading the light: SiPM photo-sensors

The detector sees through thousands of silicon photomultipliers grouped into Photon Detection Units. I characterize their response — in particular the cross-talk and afterpulse probabilities extracted from the charge spectrum — and build the baseline-reconstruction and real-time data-acquisition tools that turn their raw signals into physics.

A sense of direction: CYGNO

Before DarkSide, I worked on CYGNO, which reads out a gas TPC optically to reconstruct the direction of a nuclear recoil — the directional signature that would be a smoking gun for a genuine galactic dark matter signal.
